# Grok review: Overdrawn, chapters 1–3

Date: 2026-11-21 (Europe/Stockholm). Against `claude/webnovel-ch1-3-review` at 44670ba. Read REVIEW-BRIEF.md and STYLE.md first, then ch1–3, with pitch.md, outline.md, draft_advice.md and the project README. Chapters were not edited.

## Verdict

These three chapters work as a start. The ceremony is familiar in outline and remade in the details that matter (a pencil-written ledger on a split tally, a surety ordinance, a cost in grams, pies that are cold in the middle). Aarne is competent without being slick, the stick's rules are consistent, and the arithmetic I checked is exact. A cold reader is still asked to accept "hazel stick" and "the Notchery" before either is shown, and chapter 2's "somebody made sure" conclusion is built on a pledge book that is twenty-one years too short. Fix those. Do not sand the voice further, and do not "improve" the weight numbers; they already add up.

Word counts (body, title line excluded): ch.1 1,997; ch.2 1,819; ch.3 1,988. All sit in the author's 1,800–2,200 band. Dialogue share: 19%, 36%, 29%. Close third, past tense, throughout.

## What works (leave it)

- Chapter 1 opens on a verdict in line one, Teele cheering for the worst result. That is the right first screen. Aarne's want (three cuts, a clerk's desk) is now on the page before the machine lecture; keep it there.
- The Split lands around word 760; the rules notice around word 920. Close enough to the brief, and earlier than a late-chapter drop. The ledger lines are better than a bracketed system box. Hold them to about this length.
- Cost rule, checked: Rat-Whistle is one cut raised seven to eight, 25 g × 7 = 175 g/s; six seconds = 1,050; 52,140 − 1,050 = 51,090. Bale-Heft is four raised four, 100 g/s; ten seconds = 1,000. Two days at ~100 g eaten back: 51,090 + 200 − 1,000 = 50,290. Floor 40,000 leaves about twelve kilos, then about ten after the quay. Do not rewrite these figures.
- Aarne asking for the Pending in writing, and using "to be altered by no one but a bench" against Lind in chapter 3, is the character doing his job. That payoff should stay.
- Chapter 1 now ends on the blank `Creditor:` line. Chapter 3 ends on `Accept?`. Both are reasons to click. The rime is finally seen from the quay, which is the right time; trim the lecture around it, not the glimpse.
- Names are grounded (Rõuk, Kallas, Pärn, Rebane, Oja, Orrova). Details belong to this port: wet wool, a tin watch with one hand, PENDING burned into bark, a belt hole stitched by his mother. Loud Teele against plain Aarne is the right pair.
- Nothing explicit. No harem, no romance clichés, no names or furniture from the DO NOT REUSE list.

## Must-fix

1. **Ch.1** — "holding her hazel stick"
   A cold reader hits the exact term the author already flagged in the blurb, two paragraphs before the rod is described as "a hand-length rod of hazel." Name it plainly on first sight (a short hazel rod, a tally stick of hazel), or show the pushing-into-the-slot business before the name. "Cutting Hall" and "the Notchery" in the same stretch have the same problem at lower volume: they are used, then explained. Invented things should be visible before they are labelled.

2. **Ch.2** — "forty years of it" / "Or somebody made sure."
   Rebane defaulted sixty-one years ago. Teele's evidence is a pledge book the chapter has already dated at forty years. Of course he is not in it. "Nobody remembers him" is fair; "somebody made sure" is not earned. Change the evidence (an older register, a book that should go back far enough and doesn't, a torn-out page, a drawer with a card and no stick) or drop that conclusion.

## Should-fix

3. **Ch.2** — "using it since she was six"
   The Cutting is at sixteen. If the machine grants the skill, this is a broken rule. If people already have the talent and the machine only grades it, say so once, plainly. Right now a careful reader cannot tell.

4. **Ch.2** — "an eighth cut across the break" is not the last line
   The chapter's new fact is the eighth notch. The last paragraph (lamp, rats, "the stuffed pike… looking past all of them at the street") is the same softening already removed from the end of chapter 1. Move or trim that image so the cut is the last thing on the page.

5. **Ch.3** — "he could now pick up the barge"
   That oversells a four-cut lift raised to eight, and it cheapens the later beat where a stack "about as much as a cart" is worth noticing. Keep it at cart-scale, or at "heavier than he would have believed," not a barge.

6. **Ch.1–2** — "like a coin dropped into a pocket"
   The same simile does three jobs: Teele's skill arriving, the loan going in, the loan going out ("the same coin taken back out of the pocket"). Once is enough. On reuse, say the feeling plainly or pick a different clerkly image.

7. **Ch.1** — "Lind looked at the numbered drawers. He looked at them"
   "Looked" appears twelve times in chapter 1 (twenty times across the three chapters). This is the pet-word problem in item 11, not a mood. Cut or recast several, especially the doubled Lind line, which can stay as a pause without the repeat.

8. **Ch.3** — "He had eaten four pies since breakfast"
   The evening weight only works if recovery is capped at about 100 g a day (200 g back across two days, then 50,290). Four pies on the day would seem to do more, unless the cap is the point. Let Aarne notice that eating hard barely moves the beam, or the reader will think the numbers have slipped.

9. **Ch.3** — "A rime was what grew on a salt pan"
   The glimpse is good. The same paragraph then defines the creature, its origin, its purpose, and the guards' method. Chapter 2 already had Teele's shorter version. Let the figure on the pans do more of the work; keep the definition to a line, not a pamphlet.

10. **Ch.1** — "the annex had let him copy forms"
    First mention has no owner. "The Notchery's annex" on that line is enough.

## Optional

11. **Ch.2** — "a back room for the truth" (and "good intentions on hooks")
    Narrator kickers, close to the gimmick the author already rejected in ad copy. The humour is better when Teele or Lind says it. Plain the sentence; keep the pike.

12. **Ch.1** — "like a knuckle the size of a barn"
    The sound is clear; the image is not. A dry crack is enough.

13. **Ch.3** — "Two kilos less than at the Cutting"
    It is 1,850 g. He thinks in grams everywhere else. "Almost two kilos" would do.

14. **Ch.2** — "It wasn't brass. It was half a stick."
    One negative parallelism, contrast is real, so it is inside item 12. It is also the most recognised tell. Reword if you want this early stretch at zero; not required.

15. **Ch.3** — "That's the second time I've been owed damages."
    We have not seen the first. Cut "second time," or let it point at something already on the page (the rats, the sack).

16. Day 2 is a complete gap between the night test and "the morning of the third day." A half-sentence that he did not borrow again would close a loophole without adding a scene.

## The three hard rules

**Nothing basic.** The spine is still a sixteen-year-old measurement ceremony, a hidden grade, a system-like rule set, and a seven-day clock. The furniture on that spine is not stock: no SSS ranks, no gacha, no transmigrator, no status box. I would not scrap the ceremony. I would watch that chapters 4–10 do not add the next layer of genre default (shops, levels, a rank table, a convenient System voice).

**No gimmicky style.** The rejected blurb voice is mostly gone from the narration. What remains is a few cute narrator lines (item 11) and some dialogue punchlines ("That's just friendship with paperwork," "I get a receipt"). The dialogue ones are Teele and Aarne; I would leave them. The narrator ones I would flatten.

**Clarity.** After the early labels in chapter 1, most invented things are described as they arrive: a Split, Pending, a surety, a rime as a crusted salt figure, kroons as money you earn and owe. The numbers on the stick are explained in the same breath. The remaining holes are must-fix 1, should-fix 3, 8, 9 and 10, and the loose "two kilos."

## Webnovel pull

Chapter 1 would keep me past the first screen. The dip is the long machine-and-ranks paragraph (~160 words) before Aarne touches the slot (word 586). It is near the brief's 25–30% exposition cap, not clearly over it. I would split that paragraph through the scene rather than front-load it, but that is a trim, not a rewrite, and the author's pace can stay leisurely if the labels are fixed.

Endings: chapter 1 and 3 make me click. Chapter 2 will too, once it actually ends on the eighth cut.
