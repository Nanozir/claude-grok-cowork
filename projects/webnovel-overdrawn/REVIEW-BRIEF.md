# Review brief: what the drafts must satisfy

## Project
Original webnovel by NanPan for Webnovel. Genre picked by the author: Webnovel-style system / "SSS-talent" progression fantasy, held to Royal Road progression-fantasy standards (competent lead, earned power-ups with real costs). Approved pitch: pitch.md.

## Market/craft findings (from research)
- Fantasy holds 6–9 of every Webnovel Power top 10; hits open with an awakening/measurement ceremony, a hidden-grade talent and a system.
- Top chapters run 1,100–1,900 words (median ~1,500); the author asked for ~2,000 (1,800–2,200 acceptable).
- Hook: open on a verdict, system message or death within the first 5 lines. Reveal the power by ~800 words or as ch.1's final beat.
- Pacing: 1–2 scenes per chapter, one location each; exposition ≤25–30% of ch.1; at least one of ch.1–3 at 15–30% dialogue.
- Chapter endings: land a new fact, timer or threat (resolve-only endings read weak).
- POV: close third person, past tense.
- Readers praise: hidden-grade power, system rules with quests/costs, an early unexplained mystery, a subverted humiliation opening, a competent lead.
- Readers complain about: a lead acting stupidly, arbitrary system seals, cheap upgrades, info dumps, "numbers go up" grinding, a lead overpowered too fast, harem padding, repeated pet words, continuity and tense slips.
- Full craft data is not in this repo; the points above are the summary.

## Author's direct feedback during this project (binding, high priority)
1. NOTHING BASIC. The author always takes a non-standard route (in their own isekai they refused stock fantasy races, invented their own and remade the rest). No stock genre furniture used as-is; conventions must be invented or remade so they're unrecognisable in their details.
2. NO GIMMICKY STYLE. The author rejected lines like "No harem. No romance padding. One loud friend, a lot of debt law, and pies." and "Aarne is a bookkeeper, not a hero, so he reads the contract. Then he audits whoever wrote it." — i.e. no cute staccato fragment runs, punchline kicker lines, quippy tricolons, "X, not Y" reversals. Plain, confident prose; humour from character and situation.
3. CLARITY. The author said of the first blurb: "No idea what it's about. What is a hazel stick? One nick to seven? Twins? Writing in pencil?" A reader who knows nothing must always understand what is happening. Invented things must be described plainly before (or instead of) being named; few invented terms; no unexplained numbers.
4. Inspiration from the author's own stories is allowed for themes/tone/dynamics only — never their names, worlds, plots or lines (see the DO NOT REUSE list in STYLE.md).
5. Nothing explicit.
