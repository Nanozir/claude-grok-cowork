# Overdrawn: The Split Stick (outline, 10 chapters)

Fixed rules, stated the same way everywhere. Each person gets one skill from the machine at sixteen; the cut count (1 to 7) is its strength, and the count also sets the trade the Notchery licenses. A Split gives Aarne no skill of his own. He may borrow any skill he has watched someone use in the past hour, at strength eight. The lender cannot use the skill while it is lent. The price is his body weight: 25 grams per second for every cut the skill is raised, charged only for seconds in which the skill is doing something. He cannot borrow below 40 kilos; he starts at 52,140 grams and regains about 100 grams a day by eating. The account must be settled by the end of the seventh day (the Cutting is day 1; the deadline is sunset on day 7). Settling needs a surety, which is a promise to pay 400 kroons backed by a registered household. If it fails, the Notchery collects: the cost from the family, and the body, which the register calls "forfeit".

Weight track: day 1, 52,140, then 51,090 after the Rat-Whistle test (6 s at 175 g/s). Day 3, 50,290 after ten seconds of Bale-Heft at 100 g/s. Day 4, about 50,400, then 48,900 after 30 s of the Auditor's Grip at 50 g/s. Day 6 morning, about 49,100; the night at the pans burns about 9,000 grams, leaving 40,110.

Planted early (ch1-3): the pan bell ringing twice, rimes, the pamphlet nobody reads, the numbered drawers behind Lind's screen, the receipt's "no one but a bench" clause written by Lind, the blank "Creditor:" line, and the extra eighth cut on Rebane's half-stick.

Rests fall in chapters 5 and 8. Escalation: test (2), first use in public (3), a trap offer (4), archive trespass (6), a fight with an Auditor (7), the pans at night (9), settlement (10).

1. A Stick That Splits. At the Cutting in the salt port of Orrova, Aarne hopes for three cuts and a clerk's job. His stick splits and the machine keeps the other half. The stick tells him he has no skill of his own but can borrow, at a price taken from his body, and that the account must be settled within seven days or the Notchery collects. Hook: a line reading "Creditor:" with nothing after it.

2. The Price of a Whistle. With Teele he tests the rules on her one-cut Rat-Whistle in her aunt's pawnshop and learns the rate, the 40-kilo floor and that the lender loses the skill meanwhile. The test costs a kilo and leaves him dizzy and cold. One of the rats he called brings a half-stick marked Rebane, E., the last Split; the stick says Rebane defaulted on day seven, and the pawnshop book has no record of him. Hook: Rebane's half has the same extra eighth cut as his own.

3. Calling the Debt. Aarne goes with Teele to the Salt Quay to read a bale-man's debt aloud, and watches the man work so he will have a skill ready if she needs one. When the bale-man seizes her, Aarne borrows his lift, frees the man's foot and holds a falling stack off a barge-boy. Ten seconds costs a kilo. That evening Lind arrives with an order to suspend his Pending; the clause Lind wrote into the receipt, "to be altered by no one but a bench", stops it. Hook: Madam Oja offers to be his surety, with conditions.

4. Conditions. At Oja's counting-house Aarne counters her draft: she wants to name the people he borrows from and, on default, to take what is left of his body. He rewrites clause after clause until he reaches one he cannot change (her people may use skills in front of him whenever she says). Leaving, he is goaded into using a skill in public; he refuses, and a pan guard is hurt instead. Hook: Oja says Rebane refused her too, and she remembers the day.

5. A Market Day (rest). A quiet day at the pie cart: Hilja feeds him, Liis keeps a ledger of his weight and Teele collects small debts. Aarne finally tells his mother what the stick says about forfeit, and she tells him how his father died, and that Arvo had asked her to keep his annex key. Hook: the key still fits the annex, and Lind's night watch changes on the night before the bench.

6. The Annex After Dark. Aarne uses his father's key and clerk habits to go through the numbered drawers. He finds Rebane's drawer holding only a card: "account transferred; creditor not the Notchery", with a note about a surety "by households" signed jointly (the precedent he will need). Hook: a second lamp in the stacks.

7. Competitive Interest. The second person is an Auditor, a Notchery officer with a six-cut Grip who has been told to catch him. Aarne borrows the Grip, which costs fifty grams a second, and turns it on the Auditor, who loses it while Aarne holds it. He gets away down the stairs, thirty seconds of use costing him fifteen hundred grams, and meets Lind coming up. Hook: Lind says the Auditor will testify at the bench in the morning.

8. The Bench (rest, with consequences). The hearing is slow and bureaucratic: three clerks, the receipt and a recess for lunch. Lind has the matter put off to the settlement day, and the Auditor, who cannot use his Grip yet, loses credibility. Aarne and Teele start gathering promises for a joint surety, five kroons from each of eighty households, from people he has helped or who owe Reet. Hook: the pan bell rings three times, and the guards are out of salt.

9. The Pans at Night. On day 6 a pack of rimes (crusts of salt shaped like the debtors who died out on the pans) walks toward the Quarter. They stop at doors that carry the mark of a lodged surety, which Aarne has read in the annex. He borrows from watched guards and bale-men to move people from unmarked doors to marked ones, and cannot save everyone: he spends about nine kilos of himself and ends at 40,110 grams. Hook: the rime at the pie cart has a face, and the face is on a drawer card he saw.

10. Settlement. At sunset on day 7 the Notchery bench hears the joint surety: the eighty households' marks on one page, backed by Reet's shop, so the Quarter's promises are legally a house. Lind accepts it, and the Split is confirmed. The stick finally fills in the empty line: Creditor: Rebane, E., entered as dead for sixty-one years. Hook: the stick adds a first instruction, "come to Pan Nine."
