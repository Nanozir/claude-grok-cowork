## Ranked suggestions

1. **End ch1 on the blank "Creditor:" line.** Reason: 17 of 21 captured chapter endings land on a reveal, new fact or threat, and the blank line is the strongest one in your three chapters; the closing paragraph (folding the coat, eating the pie, the mother not yet asking) comes after it and softens it. Fit with your rules: no kicker line is added; the change moves the mother beat to before the stick is read, or trims it to a half-sentence, so the last thing on the page is the empty line. Reword rather than cut the pie-cart beat.

2. **Let one rime be seen on the page by the end of ch3.** Reason: seven of 21 endings are a threat or creature in view (Devour Monsters ch3, Shadow Slave ch2-3, Reborn in hell ch2, Supernatural Entity ch2-3, Summoning Otherworldly Women ch3), and risers use them often. Your first three chapters carry the threat through the bell, the rats and the deadline, all of it indirect. A single glimpse of a salt-crust figure on the pans from the Salt Quay in ch3 (the pans are already in view) gives readers the invented creature before ch9 pays it off. Fit: your own invention, described plainly, no combat.

3. **Bring Aarne into focus earlier in ch1.** Reason: all eight sampled first chapters keep the lead in the foreground from the first page, while your ch1 spends its first 160 words on Teele and Lind and meets Aarne as "next in line". Fit: keep Teele's opening, because it is a non-stock way into a talent test, and add one plain sentence about what Aarne wants (three cuts and a clerk's desk) inside that stretch.

4. **Trim ch1 by about 200-300 words (optional).** Reason: ch1 is 2,026 words against a ch1 median of about 1,580 and a sample maximum of 1,903. The best candidate is the dense paragraph explaining the machine and the cut ranks, which could be split across the scene rather than delivered at once. Fit: clarity stays, since each invented thing is still described plainly before it is named. Your standing preference for leisurely pacing means this is a judgement call, and it ranks low for that reason.

5. **Add "Weak to Strong" to the pitch tags.** Reason: three of the eight sampled titles carry that tag in the Research brief table (Shadow Slave, Devour Monsters, Supernatural Entity), and Aarne begins with no skill of his own. Fit: it is a search tag only and does not change the premise. You asked me not to edit the pitch, so this is advice only.

6. **Plan the posting rhythm before launch.** Reason: the brief's table lists the hits at about 7.6-14.9 chapters a week and the risers at 10.7-58.9 a week, and the earlier study found irregular updates a top reader complaint. Your outline has ten chapters, so a launch with all ten ready and several posted per day matches the riser pattern. Fit: no effect on the writing.

7. **Keep the ledger-style notices and hold them to about seven lines a chapter.** Reason: only 2 of 8 books use heavy notice text, and Goddess Summon ch3 (20 lines in 1,867 words) is the chapter earlier readers described as dull. Your ch2 block is already at seven lines, so new mechanics should arrive in dialogue or action. Fit: this keeps your own wording and avoids the standard "[Ding!]" box.

## Where following the market would conflict with your direction

- **Stock tropes and furniture** (SSS ranks, summoned goddesses, gacha draws, harem tags, transmigrators, bracketed system boxes): the risers lean on these, and none of them is needed for the structural points above. Recommendation: keep your own invention (the Cutting, the split stick, ledger notices, the rimes).
- **Reveal the power within 800 words:** the earlier study advised it, and the live data contradicts it, because two risers withhold the lead's power through three chapters. Recommendation: keep your current timing, which is earlier than most of the sample.
- **Heavy system output and notices:** common among the 2 of 8 books that use them. Recommendation: keep your lighter ledger notices.
- **High dialogue:** your chapters sit above the medians but within the range of Shadow Slave and Supreme Magus ch1. Recommendation: no change; the dialogue carries exposition, which fits your clarity rule.
- **Not measured:** point of view and tense, and reader sentiment for the eight books. The earlier study's close-third-past finding remains unverified for this sample.
